## 7. Authority Boundary

No statement in this packet constitutes certification.

The repository, RG-003 application, photographs, video, field log, or automated diagnostics cannot independently establish competency or certification authority.

The authorized governance body must make the consequential decision.

## 8. Red-Team Questions

Before approval, the decision-makers should explicitly answer:

1. Does the competency descriptor trace to an authoritative CAMPRServ mandate?
2. Is the proposed performance genuinely within CAMPRServ ownership?
3. Can the TCI be observed and assessed consistently?
4. Is the pass condition sufficiently objective?
5. Is the minimum evidence proportionate to the claim?
6. What safety/compliance conditions must apply?
7. Who is authorized to evaluate?
8. Who is authorized to certify?
9. What happens when evidence is insufficient?
10. What happens when the candidate disputes the decision?
11. What version controls apply to future assessments?
12. What external authority, if any, is involved?

## 9. Decision Outcomes

The authorized governance process should select one:

- **APPROVED** — competency and TCI become canonical under the assigned identifiers and version controls.
- **APPROVED WITH REVISION** — specified changes required before production adoption.
- **DEFERRED** — additional governance evidence or policy work required.
- **REJECTED** — proposed competency/TCI does not establish an appropriate CAMPRServ standard.

Until one of these decisions is recorded by authorized governance, status remains:

**PROPOSED / PENDING AUTHORIZATION**

## 10. Architectural Finding

RG-003 has demonstrated that physical-world field work can produce reconstructable, traceable evidence through the digital evidence system.

The remaining bottleneck is governance, not software capability.

Therefore the next implementation action should be determined by the governance decision rather than by adding another unvalidated software feature.
