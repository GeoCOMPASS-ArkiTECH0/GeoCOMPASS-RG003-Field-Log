# GeCCO 14-Unit Crucible & Red-Team Test v0.1

**Purpose:** Stress-test all 14 canonical GeCCO units against the common certification architecture without changing their canonical acronyms or descriptors.

**Governing rule:** The acronym/descriptor is the foundation. Downstream competencies, TCIs, CERTWORX, assessments, evidence, KPIs, certification rules, and GAIA-Zer0 workflows must remain traceable to that foundation.

**Fulcrum Principle:** Find the fulcrums. Leverage the inputs. Maximize the return.

## Common Crucible

Every unit is tested through:

**CANON → COMPETENCY → TCI → CERTWORX → ASSESSMENT → EVIDENCE → KPI → CERTIFICATION → INTEGRATION → GAIA-Zer0 → FULCRUM**

### Red-Team Questions

1. Does the downstream design remain faithful to the exact descriptor?
2. Are target competencies observable rather than merely descriptive?
3. Can TCIs be objectively assessed?
4. Does CERTWORX build toward demonstrated certification rather than merely course completion?
5. Is the minimum credible evidence sufficient?
6. Can the KPI be measured with a defensible formula and data source?
7. Could the KPI be gamed or improve while the real outcome worsens?
8. Does the unit have a clear competency owner without duplicating another unit?
9. Can normal workflow operate without unnecessary central intervention?
10. Does the design satisfy the Fulcrum Principle?

## Unit Results

**Crucible result:** PASS WITH IMPLEMENTATION GAPS

## Cross-Unit Red-Team Findings

### Systemic PASS

- Canonical descriptor primacy is structurally protected.
- TCI and KPI are conceptually separated.
- The Fulcrum Principle provides an anti-complexity control.
- GAIA-Zer0 is subordinate to human institutional authority.
- The Competency Record supports a single source of truth.
- CERTWORX provides a distinct certification-work layer.

### Systemic Gaps

1. **Competency Ownership Matrix** — each competency needs one authoritative unit owner; supporting units may consume it.
2. **TCI Rubric Standard** — every TCI needs observable criteria, conditions, evidence, and pass conditions.
3. **Minimum Evidence Standard** — establish the least evidence necessary for defensible verification.
4. **KPI Validity Test** — test every KPI for gaming, proxy failure, and decision usefulness.
5. **KPI Record Standard** — formula, unit, source, collection method, baseline, target, threshold, owner, action, version.
6. **Assessment Version Lock** — record the exact unit/competency/TCI/rubric versions used at assessment.
7. **Lightweight Appeal Workflow** — disputed assessment → authorized review → decision → record.
8. **Data-Minimization Standard** — collect and expose only information required for authorized purposes.
9. **Exception-Based Administration** — routine cases flow automatically; humans intervene primarily on exceptions and consequential decisions.
10. **14-Unit Integration Matrix** — map shared competencies and dependencies without duplicating ownership.

## Crucible Verdict

**No catastrophic architectural failure identified.**

All 14 canonical units can operate within the common GeCCO architecture without changing their canonical identities.

The principal remaining work is implementation-level: making each unit's competencies, TCIs, CERTWORX, assessments, minimum evidence, and KPIs concrete and testable.

The next implementation artifact should therefore be the **14-Unit Competency Ownership + TCI + KPI Matrix**, followed by detailed CERTWORX specifications only where the matrix identifies a genuine need.

## Fulcrum Test

The final acceptance question for every proposed element is:

> **Does this produce sufficient return on inputs to justify its complexity and maintenance?**

If not, simplify, combine, configure, reuse, or remove it.

**Objective: highest return on inputs with minimum necessary complexity.**
