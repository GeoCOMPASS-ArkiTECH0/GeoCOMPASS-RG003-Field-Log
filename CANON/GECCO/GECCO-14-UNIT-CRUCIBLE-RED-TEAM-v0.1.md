# GeCCO 14-Unit Crucible & Red-Team Test v0.2

## Purpose

Stress-test all 14 canonical GeCCO units against the common certification architecture, implementation controls, and future Florida/federal accreditation-readiness requirements without changing their canonical identities.

**Governing rule:** The exact acronym/descriptor is the foundation. Downstream competencies, TCIs, CERTWORX, assessments, evidence, KPIs, certification rules, external-authority mappings, and GAIA-Zer0 workflows must remain traceable to that foundation.

**Fulcrum Principle:** Find the fulcrums. Leverage the inputs. Maximize the return.

## Common Crucible

Every unit is tested through:

**CANON → COMPETENCY → TCI → CERTWORX → ASSESSMENT → EVIDENCE → KPI → CERTIFICATION → AUTHORITY MAPPING → INTEGRATION → GAIA-Zer0 → FULCRUM**

### Red-Team Questions

1. Does the downstream design remain faithful to the exact descriptor?
2. Are target competencies observable rather than merely descriptive?
3. Can TCIs be objectively assessed?
4. Does CERTWORX build toward demonstrated certification rather than merely course completion?
5. Is the minimum credible evidence sufficient without creating evidence bloat?
6. Can the KPI be measured with a defensible formula and data source?
7. Could the KPI be gamed or improve while the real outcome worsens?
8. Does the unit have a clear competency owner without duplicating another unit?
9. Can the normal workflow operate without unnecessary central intervention?
10. Can the assessment event be reconstructed later from its locked versions and evidence?
11. Are Florida requirements potentially implicated by this unit?
12. Are federal requirements or external standards potentially implicated?
13. Can the unit generate accreditation/audit evidence through normal operations rather than duplicate paperwork?
14. Does the design satisfy the Fulcrum Principle?

## Cross-Unit Red-Team Findings

### Systemic PASS

- Canonical descriptor primacy is protected.
- TCI and KPI are separated conceptually.
- CERTWORX provides a distinct certification-work layer.
- The Fulcrum Principle controls unnecessary complexity.
- GAIA-Zer0 remains subordinate to human institutional authority.
- The Competency Record supports a single source of truth.
- Accreditation readiness can reuse ordinary GeCCO records.

### Systemic Controls Required

1. **Competency Ownership Matrix** — one authoritative owner per competency.
2. **TCI Rubric Standard** — observable criteria, conditions, evidence, and pass conditions.
3. **Minimum Evidence Standard** — least credible evidence necessary.
4. **KPI Validity Test** — gaming, proxy failure, relevance, reliability, decision utility, and measurement economy.
5. **KPI Record Standard** — formula, source, frequency, baseline, target, threshold, owner, action, version.
6. **Assessment Version Lock** — exact standards used at the assessment event.
7. **Lightweight Appeal Workflow** — dispute → review → decision → record.
8. **Data-Minimization Standard** — minimum necessary collection, retention, transmission, and disclosure.
9. **Exception-Based Administration** — automate routine workflows; escalate exceptions and consequential decisions.
10. **14-Unit Integration Matrix** — shared competencies, dependencies, data, prerequisites, and external requirements.
11. **Accreditation Authority Mapping** — Florida/federal/occupational/professional/educational recognition boundaries and evidence requirements.
12. **External-Recognition Safety Rule** — never imply external accreditation, approval, licensure, or recognition without formal authority.

## Accreditation-Readiness Red Test

For every unit, ask:

> **Could an external reviewer reconstruct exactly why the competency exists, what standard was used, what CERTWORX work was required, how competence was assessed, what evidence supported the decision, who made the decision, which KPI evidence exists, what version was used, and what external authority or standard applies?**

If not, the unit remains implementation-incomplete.

## Crucible Verdict

**No catastrophic architectural failure identified.**

All 14 canonical units remain compatible with the common architecture.

The architecture should not be called implementation-ready until the 14-unit matrix work establishes concrete ownership, TCIs, CERTWORX, assessments, minimum evidence, KPIs, integration dependencies, and accreditation-readiness mappings.

## Final Acceptance Rule

A GeCCO element survives the crucible only when it is:

**descriptor-traceable + observable/assessable + evidence-defensible + KPI-valid where applicable + authority-mapped where applicable + operationally efficient.**

**Objective: highest return on inputs with minimum necessary complexity.**
